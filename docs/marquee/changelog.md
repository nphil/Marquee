# Marquee changelog

## Unreleased

- Player menus over a film no longer add their black dimming layer to the colour-conversion work, so they open and close with less graphics work. Adapted from plx-native 233a94839.
- A Library opens on the Recommended or Collections page it was last left on, kept per profile and library. Closing the page clears it. Adapted from plx-native 095f74ca9.
- Library grid cards ease down from their lift when focus leaves them, and Back to the top raises the first-row card and closes its caption band over frames instead of snapping. Adapted from plx-native 612fc8cf0, 583722c8c and 0aaf63f2e.
- Turning on an image subtitle track (the picture kind on discs) while a film plays shows the lines already read ahead at once, instead of nothing for the next ten seconds or so. Adapted from plx-native 5570be823.
- The player shows when the film or episode is expected to end ("Ends at 10:42 PM") after the time left, in the TV's 12- or 24-hour style and at the playing speed. It is hidden while paused, seeking, loading or in a trailer, and when it does not fit beside the elapsed time. Adapted from plx-native 835114dec.
- In a full-screen trailer, Up shows or hides the controls, also while the trailer is paused, and hidden controls stop asking for frames. Adapted from plx-native 17aa83ea0.
- Styled subtitles load every font attached to the film (no size cap; each font is held once however many drawing threads use it), animate busy karaoke at the video's frame rate with up to three drawing threads and a smaller draw size when needed, and keep translucent colour as authored over video through a final colour pass on the frame (adapted from plx-native b2dcd0c27).
- Glass menus (Library and Watchlist Sort & Filter, the profile menu) open and close the way iOS 26 does: the button that opened a menu is taken into it and handed back when it closes, a round lens of glass leaves the button and the menu grows from it, and closing drains the glass back into the button as a droplet. Back still closes the innermost menu, and a close no longer redraws the page underneath on its first frame. Adapted from plx-native 952d0a473.
- Settings pages slide in without a stall in their first frame: the page the focused row opens is laid out, and its text drawn, while the menu stands still. Animations step on a microsecond clock, so at 120 Hz they no longer alternate 8 ms and 9 ms steps.
- Marquee tells the LumaTV format badge which sound is playing (Dolby Atmos, DTS-HD, 5.1 and so on, as it leaves the app: a track Marquee mixes down to stereo or the server converts is reported as that), so it shows beside the picture marks. Nothing is sent when LumaTV is not running.
- Opening a page, going back and opening a glass menu no longer hold the picture for two refreshes after each capture of the page: the wait ends when the graphics chip has finished the capture. At 120 Hz every page change had three or four 16.7 ms frames.
- Settings no longer draws an unchanged page once more shortly after it slides in: laying out the next page ahead of the press used to ask for a frame.
- The who's-watching screen shows every profile's picture on its first frame. The pictures are kept ready to draw in a small file on the TV, read while the app starts, checked against the server in the background and replaced in place when one has changed, and a profile removed from the account loses its picture. The first launch after this update fills the file; later launches show the pictures at once.
- Requests opens at once: the last rows are saved on the TV and shown on the first frame, prepared with the first posters shortly after the app starts (never while a video starts) and refreshed in place without moving the selection. A poster no longer offers Request for a title just requested. "Search to request" is replaced by a "Search" pill in the page header; OK on it opens the keyboard.
- The top bar is two glass pills: the middle one keeps Home, Movies, TV Shows and Requests, and a new one at the top right holds Watchlist and Search, which Right reaches from the last middle item. Settings > Libraries > Top bar switches each of those items on or off, per profile; hiding both items of the right pill removes the pill, and Home and Settings (on the profile chip) always stay.
- Scrubbing a film at 1080p or below (H.264 or HEVC) whose server keeps no preview thumbnails now shows previews the TV makes itself: a spread over the whole film first, then stills around the scrub position, made only while playback has buffer to spare and dropped when memory runs short. 4K films still use only the server's thumbnails, and the package grows by about 0.27 MB.
- Settings > Integrations > Requests (off by default) connects Seerr, address `https://request.nateshome.net` by default, with Test connection and Forget. Once connected, a Requests tab shows Trending, Popular movies, Popular shows and Upcoming rows with status badges, searches with the on-screen keyboard and lists My requests. A title page offers Request for a movie or a season picker for a series; a title that is already requested shows "Already requested", and one in Plex shows Play. Translated in all eleven languages.
- A playback whose picture freezes recovers by itself: when the player and its source have each waited on the other for a second, playback restarts quietly at the same spot with the same audio and subtitles, and the restart is logged and counted on the Diagnostics Frames row. A wait on the server shows "Waiting for the network" after 2 seconds and offers "Press OK to play at lower quality" after 10 (for this playback only; Back puts the offer away), and a second freeze at the same spot goes straight to that offer. Skip keys on a still picture show the controls instead of jumping, and a transcode that is still starting never counts as a freeze.
- After the TV's compositor restarts (the screen server behind every app), a film that was on screen or is started afterwards no longer plays with sound over a black picture until Marquee is relaunched: the video window made on the old connection is dropped, a fresh one is made, and a playback already running reopens quietly at the same spot with the same audio and subtitles. A video window the TV refuses is retried once the same way.
- A short seek back no longer freezes the picture or plays without sound: nothing read before a seek is played after it, including when a slow seek is retried at a newer target.
- Styled subtitles that keep taking the TV more than 100 ms to draw for 3 seconds straight switch to plain text with the same words and timing for the rest of the playback, with a TV notification saying so.
- The app reads its memory use and the TV's free memory every 2 seconds off the drawing thread. While memory is short it releases artwork that is off screen, pauses artwork prefetch and holds trailer autoplay until memory recovers, and logs each change. The thresholds are first values, not yet measured on the TV.
- While the Up Next offer is on screen, the next episode's details are fetched once in the background, so Next or the end of the countdown starts it without waiting for them. The server's playback decision is still made at the start.
- Settings > Libraries > Home rows lists every row Home offers: OK picks a row up, Up and Down move it, OK puts it down, and Left or Right hides or shows it. Each profile keeps its own layout beside its pinned libraries; a hidden row stays hidden when Plex changes its hubs, and a new row joins at the end, shown.
- Settings > Spoiler protection (off by default, per profile) blurs the picture and replaces the synopsis with "Description hidden. Press OK to show." for episodes that are unwatched and not started, on season and episode lists, Up Next, Continue Watching and the episode page; OK on the text reveals one episode until the page is left. The blurred picture is fetched at thumbnail size, so the sharp image never reaches the TV.
- While a seek target is pending, Up and Down move it to the next or previous chapter start (Down first returns to the start of the current chapter when the target is more than 3 seconds into it), a skip that lands within a few seconds of a chapter start lands on it, and the seek bubble names the chapter. Items with fewer than two chapters seek as before.
- When the TV refuses a direct-play start and open recovery re-encodes the video, an external subtitle file the app draws itself (an SRT on the part) is no longer burned in by the server: the app keeps drawing it, so Timing and Style stay available. Built-in and picture subtitles are still burned.
- Deleting a movie, show or episode also takes it off the Plex Watchlist, including when the item was already gone from the server (the identity comes from the row the menu was opened on). A failed Watchlist call is tried three times before it is reported. The deletion messages are short, plain and translated in all eleven languages: "Deleted.", "Already deleted.", "Deleted, but it couldn't be removed from your Watchlist." and, for an episode whose show could not be checked, "Episode deleted. Check your Watchlist for the show."
- The launcher dock gets a native 240 px `extraLargeIcon`, so a 4K dock tile is no longer an upscaled 130 px icon. `tools/import-bazarr-link.sh` now gives the staged file the owner of the app's jail directory, which the jailed app requires to read it.
- The detail page, the info card and the audio menu label subtitles "Subtitles", subtitles for the deaf and hard of hearing "Captions" and audio description "Audio description" in plain words instead of the CC, SDH and AD marks, in all eleven languages. The Tracks panel keeps its technical labels.
- Automatic subtitles follow the audio: English audio prefers the dub transcript (an SDH/CC track, a `.en.cc.srt` sidecar, or one titled Dub/Dubtitle), other audio the full English translation, a downloaded one over a built-in one. The subtitle Plex has selected follows like any other automatic pick, an audio switch re-picks unless the viewer chose a subtitle or one remembered for the show decided it, and a playback that starts on remembered audio starts on the matching subtitle. Subtitle menus name tracks in plain words: "English", "English (dub)", "English (on-screen text)", with no format names, acronyms or file titles.
- A built-in subtitle track that a downloaded subtitle of the same language and kind duplicates is left out of the Subtitles menu; a selected or remembered built-in track, and the forced track "On-screen text only" draws, play as the downloaded twin, and a built-in track with no twin stays listed.
- The player's Subtitles menu and settings call forced subtitles on-screen text: the "On-screen text only" row and the On-screen text setting ("Show subtitles only for signs and songs"). Audio rows show the language, "(commentary)" and the channel layout, with no codec names or file titles.
- Playback preserves non-standard picture aspect ratios by default. More offers Fit, Fill/crop, Original size, Zoom and Stretch without restarting playback.
- Bazarr subtitle activation reports confirmed progress through Plex discovery and loading to Active or a typed error; metadata rechecks do not repeat downloads.
- Sidecar readiness requires a positive-duration cue and uses epoch-tagged renderer publication.
- Stats distinguishes measured and estimated rates, supports the profile menu, and offers separate session-only opt-in numeric logging.
- Stats samples native display sync separately from video and app rates, and clears unreadable or stale output rather than retaining it as live.
- Watched-state refreshes settle presented and covered detail pages, including failed reads, without leaving the loading indicator active.
- Returning from playback retains visible artwork within the existing cache limits; fast scrolling prioritizes currently visible uploads.
- Reveal as spoken can use verified SpeechDock audio timing while retaining authored cue bounds and karaoke; estimated or revised sources cannot retain stale aligned words.
- Word-by-word audio alignment now waits for the first word's verified onset, without changing the subtitle's authored interval or estimated fallback.
- Player Info distinguishes SpeechDock word intervals actually applied to the current cue from estimated or unused timing, clearing stale status across renderer and playback changes.
- SpeechDock requests preserve millisecond-authored cue bounds, skip ineligible long cues without rejecting the batch, and start correctly during the first 15 seconds of playback.
- A sidecar subtitle with SpeechDock word timing is rebuilt only when an answer changes some cue's words, not on every repeated request during dialogue, and its estimated script is reused while the subtitle style stays the same.
- Subtitle Brightness (100, 85, 70 or 55 %) dims subtitle text in every look, authored and picture subtitles included, against OLED glare. It is kept per profile, set in Settings › Subtitles or on the Color page of the player's Style menu, and shows on the subtitle as it is changed.
- A downloaded subtitle file is synced automatically when the video has a text subtitle of its own: a few looks in the first two minutes compare cue timing, and the words when the language is the same, and only a clear constant offset is applied, with a toast such as "Subtitles synced, 2.4 s earlier". Timing adjusts on top of it; a subtitle the viewer has already timed, or one SpeechDock aligns, is left alone.
- With Bazarr linked, the Subtitles menu leads with "Search for subtitles" under Off and Forced only, and the server's own search reads "Search with Plex". Once a movie or episode has played for 10 seconds the player asks Bazarr for its candidates in the background, so that search opens on its answer; nothing is downloaded or switched until a candidate is picked.
- The Size, Position and Color pages of the player's Style menu collapse the menu to a compact card at the top of the screen and take the dim and the transport off the video, so the subtitle is seen as it changes. With no subtitle on screen, a sample line stands in (size, position, colour and brightness; not font, edge or background). Back returns the normal menu.

- Dialogue Enhance uses the output audio layout and recovers playback without enhancement if the enhanced stream cannot start.
- Dialogue Enhance on an Opus track whose source fails to open no longer ends in an error: a cold start retries without enhancement, and any other failed open goes through the usual recovery (HLS fallback or rollback). A slow open is no longer reported as a Load timeout.

- Home prepares the next banner's text during idle time, moving cold text work out of banner transitions without redrawing the settled screen.
- The playback-repair help note fits in every language again (shorter troubleshooting link), and the Russian privacy title and French consent row fit their columns.
- Delete in the item menu is offered only on a server the account owns that has "Allow media deletion" on, says plainly why a delete was refused or failed, and counts an item the server no longer has as deleted. A deleted item leaves Home, Continue Watching, the Library, search, collections, people and the show and season pages at once and stays gone when the lists are read again; an episode counts out of its season and show, and a show takes its episodes with it. The on-deck episode menu on a detail page, whose Delete did nothing, now deletes.
- A short first-run tour (six pages: arrows and OK, Back, hold OK for the item menu, the player's controls, seeking, and where Settings and profiles live) is shown once per profile after the Favorite libraries screen, in all eleven languages. Back skips it; Settings → About → "Show the tour again" reopens it.
- Auto quality no longer starts at 720 kbps when the server remuxes the file (rewraps it without re-encoding): its answer to the speed check, the whole file from the start, is now used for the measurement instead of thrown away.
- A seek on a server that uses HTTP/2 no longer waits behind video already on its way: for the next two minutes the new request opens its own connection to that server instead of reusing the one still carrying the part the seek threw away.
- A seek on a webOS 4 TV whose picture never follows the new position (the clock keeps reporting the old one) now reloads once at the target after four seconds instead of staying on the old picture. Plex is not told the stale position meanwhile, and a TV that sends no frame counter lands the seek on its clock alone.
- Play starts a version the TV can decode when the first one listed is one it cannot (a 4K copy in a codec the TV lacks, or a Dolby Vision Profile 5 copy with Dolby Vision off), instead of asking the server to convert it. The quality limit is judged on the video bitrate of the version that plays, and a retry that moves to another file no longer carries the first file's audio and subtitle choice.
- The detail page's resolution badge, facts row and HDR warning describe the version Play starts (its own picture, Dolby Vision and bitrate), not always the first one listed. On a show page they describe the episode Play starts: the one being resumed, else the first one shown.
- A film played directly from the server over a link too slow for it now pauses and shows Buffering until enough has arrived to carry on, instead of playing in slow motion and then dropping frames to catch up.
- Pausing a film while the player is quietly testing a higher streaming quality no longer breaks playback: the test is dropped and the stream carries on.
- Playback that moves to another quality, or restarts at a new spot, no longer leaves a ghost row on the Plex server's dashboard: the session it moved away from is told it stopped, at the spot where playback carried on.
- When the Plex server ends a stream (an admin stopping it from the dashboard), playback now stops at once, shows the server's own reason with Back as the only action, and clears the dashboard row, instead of carrying on playing a stream the server had already dropped.
- Previous in the player works like a music app: partway through an episode it starts that episode over, and pressed again within the first 3 seconds it plays the previous episode (on the first episode it just starts over again). The button and the More menu row show which of the two the next press will do. An episode started from Continue Watching, or from a show, season or episode page, now has the episodes before it in its queue, so Previous is available there too.
- On a Magic Remote, pressing Back over the player no longer brings the controls straight back: the small movement of the pointer as the button is pressed is ignored, so a second Back leaves instead of hiding the controls again. A real move of the pointer still shows them.
- Home's backdrop (the colour wash, the hero picture and the shade over them) is drawn once while the hero stands still, instead of being re-shaded every frame, which saves graphics work. It is drawn live again whenever anything moves.
- Home's hero names up to two genres, and a show whose server gives no runtime shows its number of seasons instead.
- Home's Resume pill shows a rail for how far in the title is, and the time left, in place of the word Resume; the facts line beneath keeps the whole runtime.
- The Settings list starts with the settings used most: Home rows and Top bar under a Home heading, then Video & playback, Subtitles and Audio & subtitles, then Requests, Bazarr and SpeechDock. Language and the switches, Favorite libraries and Under posters, and Privacy, Diagnostics and About follow. From the first row, Home rows is 1 press (a press is one Down plus the final OK), Top bar 2, Video & playback 3, Subtitles 4, Audio & subtitles 5 and Requests 6; they were 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 13.

## 0.10.0

- Rosé Pine folded-M icon and splash; the package title controls display names across the app and launcher.
- Bazarr accepts the native keyboard’s literal address and key text, retains invalid drafts, and excludes typed text from diagnostics.
- Dialogue enhancement rejects unusable audio decisions and restores the previous playback route if the replacement fails.
- The first Left or Right press opens a scene-thumbnail seek preview; OK seeks and Back cancels. Instant D-pad seeking remains optional.
- The hold-menu deletes movies, episodes and whole shows after confirmation, with Plex Watchlist cleanup and explicit partial-failure reporting.
- About, support and privacy notices use fork links and current data disclosures.

## 0.9.3

- Settings > Subtitles: every part of the subtitle look as a row (preset, font, size, colour, opacity, bold,
  edge and width, background box, position, line spacing, Blend with video, Reveal as spoken, file styling),
  stepped with left and right, beside a live preview that draws sample lines with the player's own renderer.
- Settings > Integrations > Bazarr: the address and API key typed on the television, Test connection and
  Forget. The key is masked and never logged.
- File styling "My look": a subtitle file's own fonts, sizes, colours and edges are replaced by the look;
  signs and positioned lines keep the file's.

## 0.9.2

- Subtitle looks: a preset, font, edge, background and "reveal as spoken" on the player's Style pages
  (Preset, Font, Edge, Background, Reveal as spoken, Blend with video), saved per profile.
- Blend with video preset: soft translucent text that follows the scene and stays below HDR peak.
- Bundled subtitle fonts: Noto Serif, Noto Sans Mono and Atkinson Hyperlegible, plus an emoji fallback.
- "Wrong subtitles?" in the player's Subtitles menu: remove an external subtitle from Plex, or find another
  with Bazarr (score, language, provider, hearing-impaired flag), download it and switch to it.
- Bazarr address and API key stored in the protected session record; `tools/import-bazarr-link.sh` loads
  them once from a key file.
